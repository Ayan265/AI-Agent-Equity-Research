# Equity Research Report: Earnings Call Analysis

## Executive Summary
Wipro delivered a resilient performance in the face of a challenging macroeconomic environment, characterized by persistent softness in discretionary spending and delayed decision-making cycles. While sequential IT services revenue declined by 1.5% in constant currency terms, the company demonstrated strong execution rigor, expanding its operating margin by 40 basis points quarter-on-quarter to 16.4%. This margin expansion was driven by structural cost-optimization measures, improved utilization rates, and a significant reduction in sub-contractor expenses, highlighting management's focus on operational efficiency.

From an institutional investor perspective, the key highlight was the robust momentum in large deal bookings, which reached $1.2 billion, representing a 20% year-on-year growth. This indicates that client demand remains strong for cost-optimization, vendor consolidation, and cloud transformation initiatives. However, the short-cycle consulting business, particularly Capco, continues to face headwinds, dragging down overall growth. Management's strategic focus remains anchored on integrating artificial intelligence across all service lines through the Wipro ai360 initiative and driving downstream revenues from consulting engagements.

## Financial Performance
- IT Services Revenue: $2,660 million, a decline of 1.5% QoQ in constant currency.
- IT Services Operating Margin: 16.4%, reflecting an expansion of 40 basis points QoQ.
- Net Income: INR 2,835 crore ($341 million), up 1.2% sequentially.
- Earnings Per Share (EPS): INR 5.43 per share.
- Total Bookings: $3.8 billion in Total Contract Value (TCV).
- Large Deal Bookings: $1.2 billion, up 20% YoY.

## Regional Performance
### Americas 1
Americas 1 delivered a resilient performance with revenue growth of 1.2% QoQ in constant currency. Growth was primarily driven by the Healthcare and Life Sciences sector, alongside steady demand in Technology. Clients in this region are actively investing in cloud migration and digital health platforms. However, the retail segment within this geography remained soft as clients paused discretionary front-end transformation projects.

### Americas 2
Americas 2 experienced a revenue decline of 2.1% QoQ in constant currency. The region was heavily impacted by weakness in the Banking, Financial Services, and Insurance (BFSI) vertical, where large banking clients have deferred capital-intensive projects. Energy and Utilities also showed moderate softness, though there was steady traction in cloud infrastructure management deals.

### Europe
Europe revenue declined by 3.0% QoQ in constant currency, reflecting severe macroeconomic headwinds across the UK and continental Europe. The decline was most pronounced in the financial services and manufacturing sectors. Despite the overall decline, Germany showed pockets of resilience in automotive engineering services, and the pipeline for cost-reduction deals remains robust.

### APMEA
APMEA (Asia Pacific, Middle East, and Africa) recorded a modest revenue growth of 0.8% QoQ in constant currency. Performance was led by strong public sector and enterprise demand in India and the Middle East. This growth was partially offset by a slowdown in Australia, where financial services clients have significantly tightened their IT budgets.

## Sector / Vertical Performance
### BFSI (Banking, Financial Services, and Insurance)
BFSI remains the largest vertical but continues to face significant headwinds, declining 2.5% QoQ. Investment banking and mortgage segments are experiencing prolonged decision-making cycles. While discretionary spend on consulting is depressed, there is a strong pipeline of deals focused on application rationalization, legacy modernization, and cost-optimization.

### Healthcare and Life Sciences
Healthcare and Life Sciences was the top-performing vertical, growing 3.5% QoQ. Growth was driven by large-scale digital transformation contracts, regulatory compliance solutions, and vendor consolidation mandates. The pipeline remains healthy with several mid-to-large deals in the advanced stages of negotiation.

### Consumer and Retail
The Consumer vertical was flat QoQ. Retailers are highly focused on supply chain optimization, inventory management, and operational efficiency rather than customer-facing digital experiences. E-commerce investments have stabilized, but brick-and-mortar IT spend remains constrained.

### Technology and Communications
Technology and Communications grew by 1.0% QoQ. Demand was supported by telecom clients investing in 5G network monetization and cloud infrastructure. High-tech clients are rationalizing their software portfolios, leading to steady application maintenance and support opportunities.

## Large Deals & Pipeline
- **Olam Group** ($150 million): A multi-year strategic partnership to transform Olam Group's digital infrastructure. Wipro will migrate core workloads to the cloud, implement advanced cyber security protocols, and deploy AI-driven operational tools to enhance supply chain visibility and efficiency.
- **Leading Global Bank** ($250 million): A major vendor consolidation deal where Wipro will take over application maintenance and support services. This engagement is designed to drive a 20% cost reduction for the client over the next five years through automation and delivery model optimization.

## Strategic Initiatives
- Wipro ai360: A $1 billion investment over three years to integrate artificial intelligence into all service lines, platforms, and internal processes, including training 250,000 employees on AI technologies.
- Consulting Turnaround: Restructuring Capco and Rizing to align more closely with engineering and cloud delivery teams, aiming to capture downstream implementation revenues from initial consulting engagements.
- Operational Efficiency Program: Optimizing the talent pyramid, increasing offshore delivery mix, improving utilization to 84%, and aggressively reducing high-cost sub-contractor usage.

## Headwinds & Risks
- Discretionary Spend Cuts: Continued pause in short-cycle consulting projects, particularly impacting Capco's revenue contribution.
- Macroeconomic Uncertainty: High interest rates and inflationary pressures in North America and Europe delaying contract signings and project kick-offs.
- Wage Inflation: Upcoming annual wage hikes expected to create a temporary headwind on operating margins in the next fiscal quarter.

## Management Guidance
- IT Services Revenue growth for the next quarter is expected to be in the range of -1.5% to +0.5% in constant currency terms.
- Operating margins are expected to remain resilient and range-bound between 16.0% and 16.5% despite wage hike pressures.
