# Detailed Q&A Extraction

📊 **Ravi Menon** (Unknown)

    Q: Why add head count now when you're still looking at a decline in revenue next quarter?
        The head count increase includes employees who joined from the Mindsprint team. Excluding Mindsprint, the head count actually decreased by 2,500 people quarter-on-quarter.

    Q: In BFSI, we had a decline this quarter. Is this a client-specific issue, and is this something that you expect to hold us back in Q2 as well, or do you think BFSI comes back to growth?
        While BFSI declined 1.2% sequentially, it grew 2.6% year-on-year in constant currency. Europe and APMEA saw year-on-year growth, and there is good momentum and traction in the Americas as well.

    Q: Could you comment on what caused the decline in BFSI this quarter? Was there any cutback or did you lose out on any vendor consolidation?
        The decline was driven by two factors: large deals taking longer to scale and ramp up, and slower discretionary spend and decision-making. However, cost optimization and vendor consolidation remain key drivers, with clients reinvesting savings into AI capabilities.

    Q: On AI, some peers are setting up large or small capacity data centers. Are you thinking along those lines, or what is your strategy?
        Wipro is focusing on an AI-first, consulting-led, and AI-powered strategy rather than just infrastructure. This includes investing in an AI-native unit, leveraging Wipro Ventures for startups, launching innovation networks, and partnering with frontier AI companies.

    Q: Since there is not much productivity benefit in old complex code, is investor concern about significant erosion in the existing book of business unfounded?
        Yes, net-net it is a positive. While AI tools improve productivity in the software development lifecycle, it remains a 'human plus AI' model requiring human intervention for complex tasks, and AI is creating multiple new service opportunities like AI advisory, data priming, and agent management.

📊 **Nitin Padmanabhan** (Unknown)

    Q: How should we see margins recovering to the stated band of 17% to 17.5%, and do you expect a gradual recovery through the year or are there elements that could enable a faster recovery?
        Management aims to return to the 17% to 17.5% margin band but declined to predict an exact timeframe due to revenue volatility. The recent 120 bps margin drop was driven by MSI impact, investments in AI and deals, and execution of recent acquisitions. They will continue focusing on margin improvements while investing in AI-native business talent and infrastructure.

    Q: Qualitatively, is it fair to assume that the margin recovery will be gradual rather than quick?
        The endeavor is to reach the target using multiple operational levers, including automation, AI, productivity, G&A optimization, higher bench utilization, and pyramid restructuring. However, management will prioritize continuing to invest in their AI-powered and consulting-led future.

    Q: From an overall business perspective, when do you expect headwinds to recede so the business can start showing growth, and are these headwinds largely over in Q2?
        Management only provides one-quarter guidance and noted that the demand environment remains soft, as reflected in Q2 guidance. However, they are seeing positive traction in the Americas BFSI segment and have won deals in Europe's energy, manufacturing, and resources (EMR) sector.

📊 **Vibhor Singhal** (Unknown)

    Q: What is the outlook on the Healthcare vertical in terms of deals won and when will it return to growth?
        The Healthcare sector degrew by 2.6% sequentially and 3% YoY due to pressures in the US Healthcare ecosystem (payers and providers facing structural/demographic forces and flattish/negative budgets). Budgets are being reallocated to AI and compliance. Opportunities exist in reimagining processes with AI (claims, contact centers, clinical operations, regulatory compliance).

    Q: Is there any timeline for when the Healthcare vertical should see some recovery?
        Management is not forecasting beyond Q2 (where these pressures are baked in) and is not giving a specific timeline at this point, though AI presents a structural opportunity across all healthcare industry verticals.

    Q: Were the total and large deal wins down on a Y-o-Y basis due to timing, with some deals slipping into Q2?
        Yes, some decisions on deals slipped to Q2. The pipeline remains healthy with deals around cost optimization and vendor consolidation, showing strength in BFSI, Tech & Comms, and EMR, while consumer is mixed.

    Q: How should we look at the margins for large AI-driven deals (e.g., building SLMs, application layers with token costs) compared to the current portfolio?
        Margins depend on the deal structure. Deals focused on cost take-out and forward productivity will have baked-in productivity gains and remain competitive. Newer areas like Reimagine AI, data modernization, and AI advisory will drive premium rate realizations and be margin accretive.

📊 **Abhishek Bhandari** (Unknown)

    Q: Has the degree of competition in the large deal environment increased, decreased, or is it stable?
        Competition remains high, particularly in traditional IT and BPO services where client budgets are compressing. Clients are driving cost-reduction demands to free up budget for AI, leading to competitive pressures and a shift toward total cost of ownership and AI-driven productivity solutions.

    Q: How is Wipro future-proofing margins against competitive pressures and ensuring margins are not traded off for growth?
        Wipro manages margin pressures by investing upfront in traditional cost-optimization and vendor consolidation deals for long-term benefits, while capturing higher-margin opportunities in net-new 'reimagine AI' projects and programs.
